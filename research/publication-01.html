[Chinese] Revisiting Gentrification: Concepts and Applications in the Chinese Context

September 2026

In recent years, several outstanding literature reviews have systematically studied the progress of Western research on gentrification. However, when analyzing particular cases in the Chinese context, scholars always understood and used gentrification in partial or simplified ways. This article further elaborates on Western gentrification theory and points out the divergent interpretations of gentrification in the Chinese context, hoping to provide inspiration to scholars and practitioners. First, gentrification should be understood as a “process”. The theory of gentrification and the process of gentrification are two different study objects. Second, Western gentrification theory has its own limitations. It is a theory centered around the middle class and focusing on the housing market. Western gentrification theory, to some extent, limits scholars’ exploration of a broader definition of gentrification. Awareness of its limitations can help develop new theories. Finally, the current research on gentrification in the Chinese context has three limitations: First, dividing foreign scholars and their theories into the supply side and the demand side is an outdated perception. Second, case studies of gentrification are reduced to qualitative descriptions of its phenomena.
